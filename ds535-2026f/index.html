How the class runs

  • Weeks 1–8 — watch the lecture videos on your own schedule.
  • Weeks 9–16 — live online sessions for paper and project presentations.

Paper presentation

Browse the paper list↗

Team presentations, live online, starting Week 9 (10/26).

Format

  • Teams of 2 students — a few teams of 3, since 50 students share 24 papers
  • 25 minutes + 5 minutes Q&A and discussion
  • Two teams present per class
  • Cover a review of the paper plus your own research idea

Choosing your paper

  • Papers are pre-selected by the instructor — pick one from the list
  • Recent work on recommender systems or graph neural networks
  • Selection due 10/11

Paper review

Register your paper↗

You find the paper yourself, write a full review, and revise it after peer feedback.

Choosing your paper

  • A recent paper on recommender systems or GNNs, chosen by you
  • From a top-tier DM / AI / ML / NLP venue within the past 3 years — KDD, WWW, SIGIR, CIKM, RecSys, ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS, AAAI, EMNLP, ACL, and similar
  • It must not already be reviewed or summarized anywhere online.
  • Korean students write their reviews in Korean

The process

  • Paper selection — 9/20
  • Write review — 10/11
  • Peer evaluation — 10/25
  • Revise and re-submit — 11/22
  • Final peer evaluation — 12/13
Your review is read and scored by other students, so write it for that audience — reviews from past years are archived at dsailatkaist.github.io.

Project

A group project on any topic in recommender systems or graph machine learning. The goal is a research paper.

Teams

Team sign-up↗
  • 4–5 students per team
  • Team decision due 9/13
  • Any topic in recommender systems or graph ML

Deliverables

Report template↗
  • Proposal report — 10/4, 1 page
  • Progress report — 11/8, no page limit
  • Final presentation — 12/7, 12/9, 12/14, 12/16
  • Final report + source code — 12/20, max 8 pages excluding references and appendix

What the progress report must cover

  • Task — what problem are you solving?
  • Why is it hard? — why do current approaches fail?
  • Method — what are the key components of your approach?
  • Preliminary results
  • Future direction
